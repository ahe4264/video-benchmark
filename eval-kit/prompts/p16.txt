Create a narrated educational video that teaches the textbook figure 'OChem_28_04_001'

LEARNING OBJECTIVE
Analyze how the van der Waals radius of an R-group substituent dictates the steric bulk
surrounding a chiral center. Visualize the spatial consequences of an SN2 reaction by
observing the inversion of configuration when a stereochemical wedge is toggled to a
dash.
SCENE PLAN
1. Hook — Observe a 3D molecular model of a chiral bromoalkane rotating to reveal the
spatial crowding around its reactive center. This crowding dictates the molecule's
reactivity and mechanism pathway.
2. Construction — Build a central carbon atom bonded to a bromine atom, a hydrogen atom,
a methyl group, and a variable R-group initially set to a methyl substituent. The target
state displays a tetrahedral geometry with the bromine on a solid wedge and the hydrogen
on a dashed bond.
3. Demonstration — Change the R-group substituent from a methyl group to an ethyl,
isopropyl, and finally a tert-butyl group to observe the resulting increase in steric
bulk. As the carbon count of the R-group increases from one to four, the electron clouds
of the substituent expand, reducing the available trajectory angle for an incoming
nucleophile from 109.5 degrees to less than 90 degrees.
4. Demonstration — Toggle the stereochemical wedge of the bromine atom to a dash to
visualize the inversion of the chiral center. As the bond changes from a wedge pointing
out of the plane to a dash pointing into the plane, the absolute configuration of the
molecule flips from S to R, demonstrating the geometric result of a backside attack.
5. Consolidation — Review how expanding the R-group from 15 atomic mass units to 57
atomic mass units increases steric hindrance around the electrophilic carbon. Reiterate
that toggling the leaving group from a wedge to a dash models the stereochemical
inversion required in an SN2 mechanism.
