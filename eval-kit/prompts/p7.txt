LEARNING OBJECTIVE
Understand that the cross product of two vectors produces a third vector that is
perfectly orthogonal to both original vectors. Recognize that reversing the order of the
cross product yields a vector of the exact same magnitude but pointing in the opposite
direction.
SCENE PLAN
1. Hook — Finding a single direction that is perfectly perpendicular to two different
intersecting lines is a common challenge in physics and 3D geometry. The cross product
provides a direct mathematical operation to generate these orthogonal vectors.
2. Construction — Draw a 3D coordinate system and place two vectors, u and v,
originating from the origin and lying in a shared horizontal plane. Generate the cross
product vector u x v pointing straight up, perpendicular to the plane of u and v, and
then generate the cross product vector v x u pointing straight down, exactly opposite to
u x v.
3. Demonstration — Drag vector u to change the angle between the base vectors and watch
the orthogonal cross product vectors grow or shrink in length. As the angle between u
and v approaches 90 degrees, the cross product vectors reach their maximum length, and
as the angle approaches 0 or 180 degrees, the cross product vectors shrink toward a
length of zero.
4. Demonstration — Extend the length of vector v to see the u x v and v x u vectors
stretch proportionally in their respective opposite directions. Increasing the magnitude
of v directly scales up the magnitude of both cross products, making the upward vector
taller and the downward vector deeper, while maintaining their perfect 90-degree angles
to the base plane.
5. Consolidation — The cross product of any two vectors always creates a new vector
orthogonal to both, with its magnitude tied to the lengths and angle of the inputs.
Swapping the order of multiplication from u x v to v x u simply flips the resulting
orthogonal vector 180 degrees into the opposite direction.
